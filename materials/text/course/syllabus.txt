PDLC Mission Statement & Course Description:
The Professional Development: Leadership Competencies course complements Professional Development 1 /
Introduction to Engineering Design by further developing leadership competencies which students will apply in
their work as new engineering professionals. Through experiential learning, students will develop the foundations
of/= effective communication skills, emotional intelligence, cultural competency, ethical decision -making, and
critical thinking.
The experiential learning in this course will be facilitated by creating and participating in an immersive role -
playing game (RPG) using the LeadRPG model. The goal of our course’s LeadRPG is to use creativity and role-
playing to grow our own leadership sk ills and capacity within the field of Engineering, while we co -create our
immersive RPG classroom environment and engage in the game together. We will utilize the main tenants of the
LeadRPG model: Game, Learn, Lead.
Game: Experiencing Together. Our structured and imaginative play together where  story, rules,
 roles, creativity, and collaboration generate direct experiences.
Learn: Making Meaning & Growing. Our reflection on what happened, making  meaning, and
 connecting the game experience to self, group, and wider contexts.
Lead: Applying & Practicing. Our insights and action from our gameplay, as well as  learning
 experiences out of game, that we will carry into teamwork and collaborations as future Engineers.
This may be a new type of learning experience for most of you, and role -playing may be outside your comfort
zone. We will spend time as a class and in teams developing our roles and creating community norms, so we are
able to expand our comfort zones and f ully participate in the game. As your instructor, I will work to have the
intentional structures in place to guide our gameplay and make space to debrief each gameplay session. I am here
to be a resource and answer questions, so you meet your goals for thi s class. I am excited to be on this learning
adventure with you!

This course addresses the following accreditation requirements from ABET Engineering Criteria:
Engineering programs must demonstrate that their graduates have an ability[…]
• to apply engineering design to produce solutions that meet specified needs with consideration of public
health, safety, and welfare, as well as global, cultural, social, environmental, and economic factors.
• to communicate effectively with a range of audiences.
• to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed
judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental,
and societal contexts.
• to function effectively on a team whose members together provide leadership, create a collaborative and
inclusive environment, establish goals, plan tasks, and meet objectives.
Learning Objectives:
At the end of PD: Leadership Competencies, students will be able to:
• incorporate philosophical and professional ethical principles within their leadership practices,
developing appropriately professional ethical practices.
• utilize critical thinking skills to analyze professional scenarios and determine proper course of action.
• identify their strengths and weaknesses regarding leadership development competencies and articulate a
plan for growth.
• recognize the impact of social identities and social inequalities in different professional settings and
recommend actions for developing and maintaining inclusive professional environments.

Student Game Master Role
As a part of immersive RPG class experience, all students will work with a peer to be Co-Game Masters during
an in-class gameplay session. Game Master pa irs will be established in Week 2 of class, and partners will have
the opportunity to act as Game Masters for Game Days 4 – 7 outlined on the course schedule. Similar to the
LeadRPG learning experience, we recognize being a Game Master may be a new and uncomfortable role for some
students. We are here to provide guidance and give you the materials and tools you need to be successful. Your
main objective as the Game Master is to help guide your team and facilitate the journey of our RPG that week.
Instructors will be Game Masters for Game Day 3, so students will be able to observe the Game Master role
modeled. All students will receive a maximum of 40 points in the class grading scheme from an average of self
and peer evaluations from their Game Master week.

EXPECTATIONS AND POLICIES:
Assignment Policy-
All assignments are due via the method and date noted on the syllabus, unless otherwise indicated.  Students
should be in contact with the instructor as soon as possible (ASAP) if they will need to/ already have missed a
class/assignment to verify an absence will be considered excused and a make-up plan can be established.

ALL group members must be present during group presentation days .  Failure to be present during these
group activities/presentations will result in the loss of the indicated grade percentage for the assignment (s), unless
it is due to an excused absence.

Attendance Policy
Classroom attendance is required for this course.  Attendance will be taken at the beginning of class. Students are
expected to be on time and remain for the full class period.  Attendance is critical for success in PDLC. It is the
student’s responsibility to contact the instructor and to obtain any course materials missed due to excused or
unexcused absences.  Beginning the first week of class, students with three (3) or more unexcused absences
may fail PDLC. Please note that an unexcused absence on a presentation d ay will result in an automatic
20-point deduction from your final grade.

In order for an absence to be considered excused, students must provide appropriate documentation, and
obtain permission from the instructor.  If the instructor has concerns regarding a student’s success; tools may
be leveraged to assist in supporting the student.  An example may be an EWS submission for concerns relating to
but not limited to irregular attendance, missing assignments, below av erage performance on graded
assignments/quizzes.  Students are expected to attend the section for which they are registered unless they obtain
permission from their instructor.

Academic Integrity
Students are expected to maintain academic integrity.  Participation in academic dishonesty, as defined in the
current Rensselaer Student Handbook, may result in failure of the course.  All assignments outside of class
must be completed individually unless instructor specifies otherwise. All assignments must be completed
without aid from Artificial Intelligence (AI) software/programs/websites unless prior approval is granted
by the instructor. If it is found that AI was used without instructor permission to complete any assignment, the
student will receive a grade of zero (0) for that assignment. All  assignments will be submitted via LMS and put
through SafeAssign software to check for plagiarism.

Students with Disabilities
We are committed to ensuring full participation of all students in this course. If you have a documented disability
(or think you may have a disability) and, as a result, need a reasonable accommodation to participate in this class,
complete the course requirements, or benefit from the Institute’s programs or services, c ontact the Office of
Disability Services for Students (DSS) as soon as possible. To receive any academic accommodation, you must
be appropriately registered with DSS. After registration, contact your instructor as soon as possible to discuss
your accommodations so that they may be implemented in a timely fashion. DSS contact information:
dss@rpi.edu; 518-276-2231, 4226 Academy Hall.
