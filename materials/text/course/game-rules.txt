ENGR 4010 - PDLC LEAD RPG Game Rules & Mechanics
This is a cooperative narrative system. We play by co-creating our world and using
a D20 die system to help us understand the consequences of decisions and
experience navigating ambiguity and uncertainty – both are important leadership
competencies!
These game rules are a foundation, and as we build our world, we will add, adjust,
and clarify these rules together.
Unless specified before the game, no use of phones or computers while in
gameplay.
The Dice: This game primarily uses a twenty-sided die (D20).
Skill Checks: When a character attempts an action that has a chance of failure or
varying degrees of success, they perform a skill check. This involves rolling the die
and adding any relevant bonuses (e.g., a skill/leadership competency or
equipment bonus).

Setting Difficulties: As the GM, you will set a difficulty for actions. This is a
number that players must meet or exceed with their dice roll to succeed. If they
get close but don’t succeed, it is engaging and fun to give them a partial success
that comes with a twist. You want to avoid situations where the result is “nothing
happens” or they don't get any new information.

Rolling the Dice: When a player declares an action, the facilitator prompts them
to roll the die and add any applicable modifiers. Compare the total to the
difficulty you set.

Success with a Boon: If the total exceeds the difficulty by 3 or more, the action
succeeds and includes a major benefit.
Near Success: If the total meets the difficulty, the action comes close and ends up
succeeding

PAGE 3
Near Miss: If the roll is close to the difficulty but not quite there, only 1-2 off,
consider a partial success where the action gets some kind of result but with
some complication, or different than what the player intended.
Fail with a Twist: If the total is less than the DC by 3-4, the action does not go as
intended. The action itself fails, but there are other minor benefits that may
happen as a result or in addition to the action failure.

Fail with Consequences: If the total is less than the DC by 5 or more, the action
fails with a major complication. Use this to make things interesting by presenting
unintended consequences or presenting new challenges that still move the story
forward and engage the players more than being punishing

Interpreting Results: Use the results of the dice rolls to narrate the story.
Describe the outcomes vividly to keep the players engaged.
Example: If a player rolls a 17 to use a microscope and the difficulty was 15, you
might say, "You peer through the microscope and clearly see evidence of life
swarming in the sample, confirming your suspicions. And what’s more, you notice
something else…”
Example: If a player rolls an 8 to get information from a non-player character
(NPC) and the difficulty was 10, you might say, "Despite your best efforts, they
remains unconvinced and refuse to share any information. But, you notice out of
the corner of your eye that they keep glancing over towards…"

Encouraging Creativity: If players come up with creative actions, reward them by
lowering the difficulty or incorporating their idea somehow even if the dice fail.
This encourages out-of-the-box thinking and makes the game more dynamic.
